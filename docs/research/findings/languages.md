# Findings: languages and bilingual use

## Bilingual layouts

- **Keep core words in the same places across languages, and switch in one tap.** AssistiveWare's Crescendo and TD Snap's bilingual Motor Plan page set do this ([AssistiveWare](https://www.assistiveware.com/blog/bilingual-aac), [TD Snap cards](https://download.mytobiidynavox.com/Snap/documents/Training%20Cards/TDSnapMotorPlan_Bilingual_Spanish-English_TrainingCards_en-US_1000551_WEB.pdf), [QuickTalker](https://quicktalkerfreestyle.com/blog/bilingual-aac-dual-language-learners/)).
- **But an identical grid can break a language's word order and agreement** ([Avaz](https://avazapp.com/blog/why-bilingual-aac-needs-more-than-just-translations/)), and even LAMP WFL's Spanish/English set doesn't put every word in the same spot.
- **Mixing languages in one message is normal** for bilingual speakers, and should be allowed.
- These are vendor and practitioner sources; we found no controlled study of bilingual layouts.

## Spanish

From Soto and Cooper's early Spanish vocabulary for AAC ([2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2021.1881822)), built from words used by Spanish-speaking children aged 11–49 months and the Spanish MacArthur-Bates inventory (IDHC):

- **Word lists don't transfer between languages.** "Lists from one language cannot and should not be assumed to transfer to another language."
- **Children mostly use conjugated verbs** (85% of verbs in their data), mostly present tense and first person singular, and "seldom use the infinitive". Recommended forms: present, simple past, periphrastic future (_voy a…_), gerund, participle.
- **High-frequency early words** include _mamá, papá, agua, pelota, dar, mirar, tomar, yo, me, mi, mío, esto, aquí, ahí, mucho, no, grande_, and _voy, dame, mira_ among verbs; _ya_ is among the earliest words.
- **Pronoun + verb is rare** because the verb carries the person; "yo + gusto", seen in some AAC software, is ungrammatical. Most early sentences put the verb first.
- Their 218-word list is a starting pool, to be combined with what matters to each child and their dialect.

**What we do:** board words, voices and menus per language; one-tap switching; mixed messages; Spanish word choices and verb forms follow Soto and Cooper. The English-first board's Spanish is a translation for bilingual communicators; Spanish-dominant communicators get a vocabulary chosen in Spanish from Soto and Cooper's list ([0004](../decisions/0004-spanish-first-vocabulary.md)).
