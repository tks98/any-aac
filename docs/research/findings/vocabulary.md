# Findings: vocabulary and layout

What the evidence says about which words to offer and where to put them, and what Any AAC does with it. Background: [foundations.md §2.1–2.3](../foundations.md#2-what-an-aac-system-needs-field-consensus).

## Core and fringe

- A small set of high-frequency **core words** covers most of what people say (about 80% of everyday utterances in most estimates). Core lists typically run to 200–300 words across word classes ([Soto & Cooper 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2021.1881822), reviewing the field).
- **Request-only vocabularies fail.** People need to comment, ask, refuse and tell ([foundations.md §2.1](../foundations.md#21-a-full-vocabulary-core-plus-fringe)).
- **Validated starting lists:** Project Core's [Universal Core](https://www.med.unc.edu/ahs/clds/universal-core-vocabulary/) (36 words, CC BY 4.0) and Banajee, DiCarlo and Stricklin's toddler core ([2003](https://doi.org/10.1080/0743461031000112034)).
- **Core alone isn't enough for early communicators.** About 80% of the words on the MacArthur-Bates inventory aren't on young children's core lists; children starting out mostly use content words about people, things and actions that matter to them ([Laubscher & Light 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2020.1737964), cited by Soto & Cooper).

**What we do:** the core board is Universal Core plus six words ([0003](../decisions/0003-core-vocabulary.md)); topic boards and photos carry content words. The real vocabulary is still to be designed and scored with SLPs (RELAAACs).

## Motor planning and grid size

- **One place per word.** Automatic selection depends on it; grow vocabulary by revealing hidden words, not reflowing ([LAMP](https://www.aacandautism.com/lamp/research)).
- **Changing grid size means relearning places.** Liberator calls it learning a new system ([Liberator](https://liberator.net.au/support/education/aac/grid-sizes)). Systems that come in several sizes either keep one vocabulary with more behind folders in the smaller sizes (TD Snap Motor Plan, 30 and 66 cells) or grow it with the grid (TD Snap Core First) ([Tobii Dynavox](https://www.tobiidynavox.com/pages/td-snap-motor-plan)).
- **Start with the largest grid a person can access** ([Liberator](https://liberator.net.au/support/education/aac/grid-sizes)); choose the grid the person will need, not the one that looks easiest now, and small grids mean more presses per message ([AbleNet](https://support.ablenetinc.com/aac-education-and-resources/ableteams/understanding-aac-grid-sizes-and-motor-planning/)); beginners do better with a bigger screen and bigger buttons ([PRC-Saltillo](https://prc-saltillo.com/blog/LAMPWFL-iPhone)).
- These grid-size points come from AAC vendors (Liberator, AbleNet, PRC-Saltillo, Tobii Dynavox), not studies. We found no study comparing growing from a small grid with starting large. Thistle and Wilkinson compared consistent and changing symbol locations with preschoolers ([2018](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2018_AJSLP-17-0129)); _(unverified)_ we've only read the abstract.
- **Where a word is on screen is where the hand goes.** Our reasoning, not a finding: a board fills the same screen in every size, so a word keeps its motor plan best when its cell covers the same part of the screen. Different grid shapes can only keep that approximately (within half a cell, for our sizes).

**What we do:** locked positions, hidden words keep their cell, core words fixed on topic boards, phone layouts that follow the tablet's order and can be turned off ([0005](../decisions/0005-phone-layouts.md)), and starter boards in three sizes with each word in the same part of the screen in all of them ([0015](../decisions/0015-three-sizes.md)).

## Colour

- Colour **backgrounds** made adults find symbols faster in 60-symbol arrays, not in 16-symbol ones ([Thistle 2019](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/abs/10.1044/2019_PERSP-19-00017)). Grouping like-coloured symbols speeds search ([Wilkinson, Carlin & Thistle 2008](<https://doi.org/10.1044/1058-0360(2008/018)>)).
- Colour systems differ (Modified Fitzgerald, PCS), so colours should come from a theme ([foundations.md §2.3](../foundations.md#23-colour-coding-by-part-of-speech)).

**What we do:** word types stored on buttons, Modified Fitzgerald colours grouped by column, fill, border or no colour.
