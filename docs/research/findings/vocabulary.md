# Findings: vocabulary and layout

What the evidence says about which words to offer and where to put them, and what Open AAC does with it. Background: [foundations.md §2.1–2.3](../foundations.md#2-what-an-aac-system-needs-field-consensus).

## Core and fringe

- A small set of high-frequency **core words** covers most of what people say (about 80% of everyday utterances in most estimates). Core lists typically run to 200–300 words across word classes ([Soto & Cooper 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2021.1881822), reviewing the field).
- **Request-only vocabularies fail.** People need to comment, ask, refuse and tell ([foundations.md §2.1](../foundations.md#21-a-full-vocabulary-core-plus-fringe)).
- **Validated starting lists:** Project Core's [Universal Core](https://www.med.unc.edu/ahs/clds/universal-core-vocabulary/) (36 words, CC BY 4.0) and Banajee, DiCarlo and Stricklin's toddler core ([2003](https://doi.org/10.1080/0743461031000112034)).
- **Core alone isn't enough for early communicators.** About 80% of the words on the MacArthur-Bates inventory aren't on young children's core lists; children starting out mostly use content words about people, things and actions that matter to them ([Laubscher & Light 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2020.1737964), cited by Soto & Cooper).

**What we do:** the core board is Universal Core plus six words ([0003](../decisions/0003-core-vocabulary.md)); topic boards and photos carry content words. The real vocabulary is still to be designed and scored with SLPs (RELAAACs).

## Motor planning and grid size

- **One place per word.** Automatic selection depends on it; grow vocabulary by revealing hidden words, not reflowing ([LAMP](https://www.aacandautism.com/lamp/research)).
- **Start with the largest grid a person can access** ([Liberator](https://liberator.net.au/support/education/aac/grid-sizes)); small grids mean more presses per message ([AbleNet](https://support.ablenetinc.com/aac-education-and-resources/ableteams/understanding-aac-grid-sizes-and-motor-planning/)); beginners do better with a bigger screen and bigger buttons ([PRC-Saltillo](https://prc-saltillo.com/blog/LAMPWFL-iPhone)).

**What we do:** locked positions, hidden words keep their cell, core words fixed on topic boards, phone layouts that follow the tablet's order and can be turned off ([0005](../decisions/0005-phone-layouts.md)).

## Colour

- Colour **backgrounds** made adults find symbols faster in 60-symbol arrays, not in 16-symbol ones ([Thistle 2019](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/abs/10.1044/2019_PERSP-19-00017)). Grouping like-coloured symbols speeds search ([Wilkinson, Carlin & Thistle 2008](<https://doi.org/10.1044/1058-0360(2008/018)>)).
- Colour systems differ (Modified Fitzgerald, PCS), so colours should come from a theme ([foundations.md §2.3](../foundations.md#23-colour-coding-by-part-of-speech)).

**What we do:** word types stored on buttons, Modified Fitzgerald colours grouped by column, fill, border or no colour.
