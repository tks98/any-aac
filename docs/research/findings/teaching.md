# Findings: teaching, modeling and keeping AAC in use

## Aided language modeling

- Partners modeling on the person's own AAC system while they talk is the best-evidenced AAC teaching practice. A review of modeling interventions found gains in pragmatics, vocabulary, syntax and morphology ([Sennott, Light & McNaughton 2016](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/effects-of-interventions-that-include-aided-augmentative-and-alte/)); a meta-analysis found aided AAC input "highly effective across participants of various ages, disabilities, and language skills" ([O'Neill, Light & Pope 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29931287/)). Most studies are single-case designs.
- Partners need to find words fast to model them, and their modeling shouldn't count as the person's own words ([foundations.md §2.6](../foundations.md#26-aided-language-modeling)).

**What we do:** modeling mode with Find word: taps are said but not added to the message, and the path to any word is shown one step at a time ([0006](../decisions/0006-partner-modeling.md)).

## Just-in-time vocabulary and visual scenes

Adding vocabulary in the moment increased symbolic communication ([AJSLP 2017](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2017_AJSLP-15-0197), [2025](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2025_AJSLP-24-00450)). Any AAC lets a word with a photo be added in edit mode; visual scene displays are planned for phase 3.

## Abandonment

Systems are abandoned when they don't fit, partners aren't trained, there's no structured teaching, or they aren't updated ([foundations.md §2.9](../foundations.md#29-why-aac-systems-get-abandoned)). Fast, forgiving editing, printed backup boards, good defaults and teaching tools respond to this; training materials don't exist yet.
