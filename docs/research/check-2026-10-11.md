## Fixed in this pass

1. **Voice licences.** Our research said to check each voice's MODEL_CARD, and we checked the recordings' licences, but not what each voice was fine-tuned from. Joe and Alba (English) and Davefx and Ald (Spanish) were fine-tuned from Piper's lessac voice, whose recordings are licensed for research only, explicitly excluding "developing … voice synthesis … products" ([licence](https://www.cstr.ed.ac.uk/projects/blizzard/2013/lessac_blizzard2013/license.html)). Claude (Mexican Spanish) doesn't document its training at all. These five are now labelled in Settings, listed after clearly open voices, and never recommended. Claude is no longer the recommended Spanish voice. Linda, Kristin, Norman and Cori (English) and Carlfm (Spanish) were trained from scratch on public-domain recordings and are clear. The lessac licence doesn't say whether a model fine-tuned from lessac counts as a derived work, so the five aren't removed yet; see _Decisions needed_.
2. **Spanish words.** Soto and Cooper's early Spanish vocabulary for AAC ([2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2021.1881822)) found that young Spanish speakers mostly use conjugated verbs, chiefly the first person and commands, and "seldom use the infinitive". Its highest-frequency words include _voy_, _dame_, _mira_ and _tomar_, and _ya_ is among the earliest words. So _ir_ → _voy_, _beber_ → _tomar_ (also the usual word in Mexican Spanish), _terminé_ → _ya_. Our other choices (_quiero_, _dame_, _mira_, _me gusta_, not "yo gusto") already matched it.
3. **Spanish word forms.** The same paper recommends the present, simple past, periphrastic future (_voy a comer_), gerund and participle. The engine had all but the last two; it now has all five.
4. **One layout everywhere.** Smaller layouts on phones can be turned off (Settings → Display), so a communicator who uses both a phone and a tablet keeps every word in one place. See _Phone vs tablet sizes_ below.
5. **Speech gaps.** Firefox on Android has no speech synthesis, and some devices read a language with the wrong voice ([foundations.md §4.1](foundations.md#41-speech-output)). Settings now warns when the device has no voice for the language in use, and edit mode warns when nothing can speak until an app voice is downloaded.
6. **Language names mid-sentence** now follow each language's rules ("voz en español", not "en Español").
7. **New docs:** [setup.md](../setup.md) (installing, locking the device, choosing a voice, switches) and [feature-match.md](../feature-match.md) (our features in the categories clinicians compare).

## Decisions needed

_Outcome (2026-10-11): all four were approved. 1 → [0002](decisions/0002-only-clearly-licensed-voices.md) (the five voices removed), 2 → [0003](decisions/0003-core-vocabulary.md), 3 → [0004](decisions/0004-spanish-first-vocabulary.md), 4 → [0005](decisions/0005-phone-layouts.md)._

1. **The five voices with unclear licences.** Options: (a) keep them, labelled, as now; (b) remove them until the licences are settled; (c) ask the Piper and voice authors. Spanish is the hard part: the only clearly open Spanish Piper voice is Carlfm, which is small and low quality. Kokoro-82M is Apache 2.0, built on the public-domain LJ Speech recordings, and lists Spanish voices, including a female one (`ef_dora`), which we don't have at all ([Kokoro guide](https://soniqo.audio/guides/kokoro), [model card](https://replicate.com/alphanumericuser/kokoro-82m/readme)). It's worth evaluating as the clean Spanish voice, though it's heavier than Piper ([foundations.md §4.1](foundations.md#41-speech-output)). **Recommendation:** (b) for a public release, with Kokoro evaluated first, so nobody builds habits on a voice we might withdraw.
2. **Core vocabulary.** Project Core's [Universal Core](https://www.med.unc.edu/ahs/clds/universal-core-vocabulary/) is 36 words prioritised from published core lists, device vocabularies and frequency data, and it's licensed CC BY 4.0, so we can use it with credit. Our board has 28 of the 36. Missing: _all, different, here, same, some, turn, when, why_. Adding them means dropping 8 of our 14 words that aren't in it (_we, play, feel, is, have, eat, drink, again, big, little, bad, down, yes, no_), or adding a row. Banajee et al.'s toddler core list ([2003](https://doi.org/10.1080/0743461031000112034)) supports keeping _yes, no, is_ and adding _my/mine, here, some, out, off_. **Recommendation:** adopt all 36 Universal Core words, keep _yes, no, is_ and _eat, drink_ from our list, with the layout reviewed by an SLP. This changes the approved starter board, so it's your call.
3. **A Spanish-first layout.** Bilingual guidance agrees on keeping core words in the same places across languages and switching in one tap: AssistiveWare's Crescendo and TD Snap's bilingual Motor Plan page set do this ([AssistiveWare](https://www.assistiveware.com/blog/bilingual-aac), [TD Snap cards](https://download.mytobiidynavox.com/Snap/documents/Training%20Cards/TDSnapMotorPlan_Bilingual_Spanish-English_TrainingCards_en-US_1000551_WEB.pdf)). But word lists don't transfer between languages ([Soto & Cooper 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2021.1881822)), and an identical grid can break the other language's word order and agreement ([Avaz](https://avazapp.com/blog/why-bilingual-aac-needs-more-than-just-translations/)). Our approach suits bilingual communicators whose boards start in English; a Spanish-dominant communicator needs a vocabulary chosen in Spanish (Soto and Cooper's 218 words are the place to start) and reviewed by Spanish-speaking SLPs.
4. **Phone vs tablet.** Research says sizes should share positions. Our phone layout doesn't, so it's a second motor plan. PRC-Saltillo recommends a larger screen and bigger buttons for people starting out ([LAMP WFL on iPhone](https://prc-saltillo.com/blog/LAMPWFL-iPhone)). **Recommendation:** keep phone layouts for partners and experienced users, recommend one primary device for beginners in [setup.md](../setup.md), and design the real vocabulary so the phone set is a subset in matching relative positions.

## Detail by area

### Motor planning and sizes

Research: one fixed place per word; grow by revealing hidden words; smaller and larger sets share positions ([foundations.md §2.2](foundations.md#22-motor-planning-consistency)). Locking, hiding without moving, and fixed core positions across topic boards all follow this. (Thistle and Wilkinson have studied consistent symbol location and motor learning with preschoolers, [2018](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2018_AJSLP-17-0129); we couldn't open the article to check its findings.) The phone layouts are the exception (decision 4). Liberator advises starting with the largest grid a person can access ([Liberator](https://liberator.net.au/support/education/aac/grid-sizes)), and AbleNet notes that small grids mean more presses per message ([AbleNet](https://support.ablenetinc.com/aac-education-and-resources/ableteams/understanding-aac-grid-sizes-and-motor-planning/)).

### Core vocabulary

Our starter mixes requests with comments (_good, bad, big, little_), questions (_what, where, who_) and refusals (_not, no, stop, all done_), so it avoids the request-only failure mode. It is still hand-picked. Soto and Cooper point out that core lists suit children combining words, while early symbolic communicators also need personally meaningful content words ([Laubscher & Light 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2020.1737964), cited there), which is what the topic boards and photos are for. Decision 2 covers alignment with Universal Core. RELAAACs scoring still waits for the SLP-designed set.

### Colour coding

Part of speech is stored on each button and colours come from a theme, as recommended. Colour backgrounds made adults find symbols faster in 60-symbol arrays, though not in 16-symbol ones ([Thistle 2019](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/abs/10.1044/2019_PERSP-19-00017)), and grouping like-coloured symbols speeds search ([Wilkinson, Carlin & Thistle 2008](<https://doi.org/10.1044/1058-0360(2008/018)>)). Our 48-word board with colours grouped by column fits both. Users who find the colours busy can choose Border or None.

### Grammar, Spanish and bilingual use

The Forms button follows the grammar-popup model the research describes. One refinement is worth considering: putting the form that agrees with the subject already in the message first (_he_ → _wants_). (foundations.md says Proloquo2Go does this; its grammar-popup page doesn't say so, so that claim is unconfirmed.) Aided modeling studies also report gains in morphology ([Sennott, Light & McNaughton 2016](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/effects-of-interventions-that-include-aided-augmentative-and-alte/)), which is another reason modeling tools matter. For Spanish, see fixes 2 and 3 and decision 3. Gender agreement is offered through Forms; a per-communicator gender setting would remove a step.

### Voices

See fix 1 and decision 1. Everything else matches: optional downloadable voice, pre-rendered audio, Web Speech fallback. Every voice is adult. The research's "a voice the user is willing to be heard in" suggests children's voices, but we found no openly licensed Piper child voices; that's worth a wider search.

### Switch and auditory scanning

The engine covers the methods, patterns and timing in [foundations.md §2.7](foundations.md#27-access-methods) and accepts every input in §4.3. Missing: block scanning, two-switch tap zones (left and right halves of the screen), and scanning settings and edit mode. Auditory scanning practice uses group cues that hint at what a group holds, cues that shrink to "row 1, row 2" as people learn the layout, and private cues in headphones with a distinct cue voice ([Saltillo](https://saltillo.com/support/article/scan-prompts-8), [Ace Centre Pasco](https://acecentre.org.uk/page/pasco-support/), [Bridge School](https://communication.bridgeschool.org/intervention/aac-system-design-guiding-principles/aac-system-design-examples-of-aac-tools-and-techniques/)). We cue with each group's first word in the device voice; the cue voice and headphone routing are still to do. Browsers can only route audio to a chosen output in some browsers (`setSinkId`), so this needs testing. All of it needs testing with switch users.

### High contrast and CVI

Vision professionals value dark backgrounds for reducing clutter but warn against extreme, unnatural contrast and against colourful boxes on black; recommendations must be individual ([PrAACtical AAC](https://praacticalaac.org/praactical/aac-cvi-can-we-chat/), [Global Symbols](https://globalsymbols.com/news/2024-06-28-aac-symbols-for-individuals-with-cerebral-visual-impairment-cvi?locale=en), [TIES](https://publications.ici.umn.edu/ties/communicative-competence-tips/learners-with-cvi-and-aac)). Our High contrast theme puts symbols on white cards so their black outlines show, which may add visual complexity for someone with CVI. It stays a general high-contrast theme; a CVI preset (planned for phase 2) should be designed with a CVI specialist.

### Aided language modeling

Interventions with aided AAC input were "highly effective across participants of various ages, disabilities, and language skills" ([O'Neill, Light & Pope 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29931287/)), with gains in pragmatics, vocabulary, syntax and morphology ([Sennott, Light & McNaughton 2016](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/effects-of-interventions-that-include-aided-augmentative-and-alte/)). Most studies are single-case designs, but this is the strongest evidence base in the field, and the research named partner modeling tools as a gap in every open-source project. **Find word** (show the path to any word) and a **modeling mode** (partner taps highlighted, not spoken as the user's own) should come next.

## Since this check

_Updated 2026-10-12._ What changed for the open items above:

- **Aided language modeling (❌):** built the same day: modeling mode, where partners' taps are said but not added to the message, and Find word, which shows the path to any word ([0006](decisions/0006-partner-modeling.md)).
- **Motor planning, sizes (🟡):** the starter boards come in three sizes, with each word in the same part of the screen in all of them ([0015](decisions/0015-three-sizes.md)). Phone layouts are still a second set of places, the same in every size.
- **Voices:** an app voice is the default, because iOS mutes the device voice in silent mode ([0012](decisions/0012-app-voice-by-default.md)), and voices download at half the size ([0013](decisions/0013-half-precision-voices.md)).
- **Licensing, no NC content by default:** pictures linked from other sites in imported boards, often ARASAAC or Sclera (non-commercial), aren't loaded ([0014](decisions/0014-real-imports.md), [findings/formats.md](findings/formats.md)).
- **Privacy:** a security policy makes the browser refuse anything from other sites ([0010](decisions/0010-hosting.md), [findings/platform.md](findings/platform.md#security-policy)).
- **Not in this check, since found:** updates waited until every copy of the app closed ([0016](decisions/0016-updates-when-put-away.md)); imports had only been tested with made-up files ([0014](decisions/0014-real-imports.md)); and there was no help in the app for the adult setting it up ([0009](decisions/0009-plain-interface.md), [findings/interface.md](findings/interface.md#cognitive-accessibility)).
